JAN-SETU: USER IDENTIFICATION

Jan-Setu is designed for people who report civic problems, people who receive and manage those complaints, and organisations that benefit from a better complaint process.

The platform is built around a simple idea: citizens should be able to report a local problem without needing to understand government systems, while officials should receive clearer and more useful information.

======================================================================
1. PRIMARY USERS: CITIZENS
======================================================================

Citizens are the main users of Jan-Setu. They may need to report local problems such as potholes, drainage blockages, broken streetlights, sanitation issues, damaged public facilities, or urgent safety concerns.

Different citizens have different levels of comfort with technology. Jan-Setu supports this by offering both a web app and a guided WhatsApp flow.


1.1 LOW-DIGITAL-LITERACY CITIZENS

Example profile: Pintu Yadav, farmer and shopkeeper, Bihar

Who they are:
• People who use WhatsApp regularly but may not know about government complaint websites.
• People who may live in rural or semi-urban areas.
• People who are more comfortable speaking than typing.
• People who may find long forms and formal government language difficult.

Current difficulties:
• They may need to visit government offices several times to report a problem.
• They may spend money travelling to offices.
• They may face pressure to rely on informal payments or personal contacts.
• They may not receive confirmation that their complaint was registered.

What they need from Jan-Setu:
• A familiar WhatsApp-based way to begin a complaint.
• Simple, guided steps in understandable language.
• Ability to share their location directly from their phone.
• Ability to describe a problem using a voice message.
• Clear confirmation and an easy way to check complaint status.


1.2 DIGITALLY AWARE BUT PROCESS-UNFAMILIAR CITIZENS

Example profiles: Aditya Singh, Namrita Singh, Teena Khanna

Who they are:
• People who use smartphones, online services, and messaging apps confidently.
• Students, workers, community members, and urban residents.
• People who are willing to use a web app but may not know civic complaint processes.

Current difficulties:
• They may not know that a suitable complaint portal exists.
• They may not know which department handles a particular problem.
• They may have tried toll-free numbers or office visits but received no tracking or follow-up.
• They may be frustrated by having to repeat complaint details to different offices.

What they need from Jan-Setu:
• A simple web app for filing and tracking complaints.
• Automatic guidance toward the correct department.
• Ability to submit location, written details, voice recordings, and photos.
• A ticket number and visible complaint status.
• Fast handling for complaints that may be urgent.


1.3 TECH-SAVVY CITIZENS

Example profile: Harish Kumar, software engineer

Who they are:
• People with high digital confidence.
• People who expect clear online services and transparent progress tracking.
• People who are comfortable using maps, uploads, and web dashboards.

Current difficulties:
• They may be ready to use a civic platform but cannot identify the correct department.
• They may lose trust when systems do not explain what happens after submission.
• They may find traditional public-service portals unclear or difficult to navigate.

What they need from Jan-Setu:
• A well-designed, mobile-friendly web app.
• Clear complaint history and status information.
• A simple explanation of the category and destination of a complaint.
• A reliable way to attach evidence and review details before submitting.
• Transparency about whether a complaint is being reviewed, grouped, routed, or completed.


======================================================================
2. SECONDARY USERS: GOVERNMENT OFFICIALS AND COMPLAINT RECEIVERS
======================================================================

Government officials and complaint receivers use Jan-Setu to review incoming complaints, understand the issue, decide whether action is needed, and send cases to the right team.

They need a system that reduces manual effort without removing human judgement.


2.1 MUNICIPAL COMPLAINT CLERKS

Example profile: Sanjay Kumar, Head Clerk, Patna Nagar Nigam

Who they are:
• Municipal staff members who receive, register, sort, and forward public complaints.
• People who may handle a high number of complaints every day.
• People responsible for ensuring cases reach the appropriate team.

Current difficulties:
• The entire workflow may depend on paper registers, calls, and manual forwarding.
• They may handle around 60 to 250 complaints in a day.
• Complaints often arrive missing important information such as location, contact details, or a proper description.
• Fixing incomplete complaints can add one or two days to processing.
• They may need to manually identify the problem type and responsible department.

What they need from Jan-Setu:
• A clear official complaint queue.
• Complaints with location, contact information, and issue description already collected.
• A short system-generated summary and suggested category.
• Visible priority, safety, and evidence-related flags.
• Options to approve a route, redirect a case, ask for clarification, or reject a case with a reason.
• A record of actions taken on each complaint.


2.2 UTILITY DEPARTMENT CLERKS

Example profile: Savitur Tyagi, Clerk, Bijli Vibhag, West Midnapore

Who they are:
• Staff members working in specialised service departments, such as electricity, water, sanitation, roads, or drainage.
• People who need to receive cases that fall within their department’s area of responsibility.

Current difficulties:
• Department portals may exist but are not widely used by citizens.
• Many citizens still report problems in person.
• Staff often receive complaints meant for a different department.
• Misrouted cases waste staff time and delay action for citizens.

What they need from Jan-Setu:
• Better routing based on the complaint type and location.
• Clear case summaries before they begin work.
• Fewer duplicate and irrelevant complaints.
• Clear information when a case needs review rather than automatic routing.
• A way to receive priority cases quickly.


======================================================================
3. TERTIARY USERS: INDIRECT STAKEHOLDERS
======================================================================

These users may not use Jan-Setu as often as citizens or clerks, but the platform affects their work and helps them respond more effectively.


3.1 FIELD OFFICERS

Example profile: Sarvesh Pandey, CISF

Who they are:
• People who respond on the ground or investigate urgent public issues.
• Staff who may currently receive complaints only after they pass through several offices.

Current difficulties:
• Complaints can arrive through slow manual forwarding chains.
• Urgent cases may be delayed along with routine complaints.
• Field teams may not receive enough location or evidence information before responding.

What they need from Jan-Setu:
• Faster access to priority or safety-related complaints.
• Accurate location information.
• Clear descriptions, photos, and other supporting details where available.
• Better routing so the correct field team receives the case sooner.


3.2 INSTITUTIONAL ADMINISTRATIVE STAFF

Example profile: Vivek Kumar, Library Staff

Who they are:
• Administrative staff in institutions such as schools, libraries, public offices, campuses, or other public-serving organisations.
• People who may manage complaints inside their own organisation or coordinate with local authorities.

Current difficulties:
• Complaint handling may rely on manual records and informal follow-up.
• There may be no clear tracking system.
• Staff may not know whether a reported issue has been acted on.
• Repeated follow-ups can take time away from regular work.

What they need from Jan-Setu:
• A structured way to register and follow complaints.
• Clear status updates.
• Less manual coordination and fewer repeated phone calls.
• A visible history of what was reported and what happened next.


======================================================================
4. SHARED USER NEEDS
======================================================================

Citizens need:
• A simple and familiar way to report problems.
• Support for local languages and different levels of digital confidence.
• Location, voice, text, and photo options.
• Clear confirmation that a complaint was received.
• Easy complaint tracking without repeated office visits or calls.

Government officials need:
• Complete and structured complaint information.
• Faster sorting and routing of cases.
• Clear identification of urgent, uncertain, or duplicate reports.
• Human control over important decisions.
• A record of complaint handling and official actions.

Field and institutional staff need:
• Faster access to relevant cases.
• Better information before responding.
• Less dependence on manual forwarding and unclear follow-up.


======================================================================
5. HOW JAN-SETU SERVES ALL USER GROUPS
======================================================================

Jan-Setu acts as a bridge between citizens and public-service teams.

For citizens, it makes civic complaint filing easier, more accessible, and easier to track.

For officials, it turns incoming reports into clearer, more organised cases that can be reviewed and routed with less manual effort.

For field teams and institutions, it improves the flow of information so the right people can respond with better context and fewer delays.

The common goal is a complaint process that is more structured, accessible, transparent, and fair for everyone involved.
